## Title
Lincoln's Guard Left His Post. What If He Had Stayed? 🎩 #Shorts

## Description
The night Abraham Lincoln was shot, the policeman guarding his door wasn't there.

Washington, 14 April 1865, five days after Lee's surrender. Lincoln watches the comedy Our American Cousin from the State Box at Ford's Theatre. The Metropolitan Police officer assigned to guard the box entrance, John Parker, reportedly went to the saloon next door at intermission with Lincoln's footman and coachman; whether he ever came back is unknown. Three days earlier, in his last public address, Lincoln had said he would prefer the vote be given to Black men who were "very intelligent" and to "those who serve our cause as soldiers"; the actor John Wilkes Booth was reportedly in that crowd. Just after ten, Booth showed the footman Charles Forbes his card, slipped into the passage and barred its door with a pine bar. He fired one shot under the laugh that followed the line "you sockdologizing old man-trap!" Lincoln died at 7:22 the next morning. Andrew Johnson became president; he issued over 13,000 pardons and in 1866 vetoed the Freedmen's Bureau bill and the Civil Rights bill, and Congress overrode the Civil Rights veto. Historians still disagree on how much Lincoln would have changed. Parker was charged with neglect of duty in May 1865; the complaint was dismissed on 2 June.

Would one guard at one door have changed history? 👇
