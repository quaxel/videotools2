# Research: The Cosmic Static Trapped in Pigeon Poop

## Mechanism

In 1964, on a hill in Holmdel, New Jersey, two young radio astronomers at a telephone company lab, Arno Penzias
(born 1933) and Robert Wilson, turned a 20-foot horn antenna, built for the Echo balloon satellites, into a
radio telescope to measure faint radio signals from the Milky Way. Their receiver, at the narrow end of the horn in a
small equipment cab, kept showing a faint, steady excess of microwave noise, about 3.5 kelvin hotter than it should
have been, at 4080 megacycles (7.35 cm). It was the same by day and by night, in every direction they pointed. They
ruled out New York City and our galaxy. A pair of pigeons was nesting in the horn and had coated it with what Penzias
called a white dielectric substance; they cleaned it out and sent the birds away (where to differs by source),
but the pigeons came back two days later; what happened to them then differs by source (released 30 miles away and returned, then killed, in a 2005 NPR piece;
a technician with a shotgun in Wilson's own telling). The noise stayed. The hiss was the cosmic microwave background:
the light left over from the hot, dense early universe (the Big Bang), stretched by the expansion of space into faint
microwaves at about 3 degrees above absolute zero, filling the whole sky evenly. At Princeton, Robert Dicke's group had
predicted it and was building its own antenna to find it; when Penzias phoned Dicke, Dicke told his team "We've
been scooped." The two groups published side by side in 1965, and Penzias and Wilson shared the 1978 Nobel Prize in
Physics.

- How the pigeons finally left: a trap and removal in some accounts, a technician with a shotgun in Wilson's own
  interview (Slate / New Scientist); "each physicist says the other ordered it" in another. Sources differ, so the
  script only says the accounts disagree, and the picture never shows a gun.
- That the pigeons were mailed to a pigeon fancier, or to the company's main offices: the destination differs by source.
  The script says only "sent away".
- Dicke's "thirty miles" drive from Princeton: one source only.
- Ivan Kaminow's line "looked for dung but found gold": one source (APS) only; the closing line is in our own words.
- 13.8 billion years, 2.725 K, the 7.35 cm wavelength and 4080 megacycles: true, but not needed and too many numbers.
