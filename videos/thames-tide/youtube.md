## Title
Why the Thames Kept Bringing Parliament's Stink Back 🌊 | 1858 #Shorts

## Description
In 1858 Parliament poured lime into the Thames, and the river sent it all back.

London, summer 1858: temperatures hit thirty degrees and stayed high for weeks, and London's sewage ended up in the Thames, right under Parliament's windows. Members could not stay in their own library and committee rooms. The government poured a hundred tons of lime or more into the river at the mouths of the sewers. But at Westminster the Thames is tidal: in the words of one MP, the sewage was "bandied up and down under the noses of the citizens". The answer was a system of intercepting sewers. Joseph Bazalgette's 82 miles of brick sewers carried the sewage east, and at Crossness a reservoir holding 27 million gallons opened its sluices at high tide so the ebb carried it out toward the sea. The bill became law in just 18 days.

Would you have trusted the lime? 👇
