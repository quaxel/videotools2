# Research: The Mississippi River ran backward (New Madrid earthquakes 1811-1812)

## Mechanism

Three great earthquakes struck the central Mississippi valley around New Madrid, Missouri, then the largest
settlement on the river between St. Louis and Natchez: about 2:15 am on 16 December 1811 (northeast Arkansas),
23 January 1812 (M7.3, Missouri) and 7 February 1812 (M7.5, on the Reelfoot fault in Missouri and Tennessee). High
banks caved into the river, sand bars and island points gave way, and trees were bent and snapped. On 7 February the
Reelfoot fault pushed up the Lake County uplift (up to 10 m above the valley, bounded by the 3 m Reelfoot scarp)
across the river's path. The sudden uplift threw a wave upstream (a "fluvial tsunami") and for several hours the
current ran backward; waterfalls formed in the river and huge waves overwhelmed many boats. Then the river flowed
south again, but the land stayed changed: east of the scarp the ground sank 1.5-6 m and filled to become Reelfoot
Lake in Tennessee. USGS notes the uplift and waves "gave the illusion" of upstream flow over a reach of river, so the
script says a part of the current turned upstream, for a short time.

## Claims

- The New Madrid earthquakes struck in eighteen eleven and eighteen twelve (1811 and 1812), named after New Madrid, Missouri — https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/summary-1811-1812-new-madrid-earthquakes-sequence
- The first shock came at about 2:15 am on 16 December 1811; the strongest, M7.5, on 7 February 1812 on the Reelfoot fault — https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/summary-1811-1812-new-madrid-earthquakes-sequence
- High banks caved and collapsed into the river; sand bars and points of islands gave way — https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/summary-1811-1812-new-madrid-earthquakes-sequence
- The Lake County uplift stands up to 10 m above the valley, bounded by the 3 m Reelfoot scarp; local uplift and waves made the river seem to flow upstream — https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/summary-1811-1812-new-madrid-earthquakes-sequence
- Huge waves on the Mississippi overwhelmed many boats; trees were bent until their branches intertwined — https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/summary-1811-1812-new-madrid-earthquakes-sequence
- The 7 February 1812 quake caused a fluvial tsunami that made the river run backward for several hours; waterfalls were created in an instant; large trees were snapped in two — https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/February-7/earthquake-causes-fluvial-tsunami-in-mississippi
- Sunken ground east of the Reelfoot scarp (1.5-6 m of subsidence) became Reelfoot Lake in Tennessee — https://www.usgs.gov/programs/earthquake-hazards/science/summary-1811-1812-new-madrid-earthquakes-sequence
