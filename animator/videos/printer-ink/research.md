# Research: Why printer ink costs more than champagne

## Mechanism

Home inkjet printers follow the razor-and-blades model: the machine is priced to get it into your home and the
money is made on the ink you keep buying. Consumer Reports (2013) priced ink at about $13 an ounce for the cheapest
and up to $75 for the dearest, so even the cheapest ink costs more per ounce than fine champagne. Cartridges carry
authentication chips; in September 2016 one maker's firmware began rejecting cartridges without its official chip.
Printers also spend ink on maintenance: in Consumer Reports' intermittent-use test some used 50 % or more of their ink
for non-printing functions such as head cleaning, and in a few only 20 to 30 % reached the paper. In January 2024 a
printer-company CEO told CNBC that every printer a customer buys is "an investment" for the company, and a customer
who does not use its supplies is "a bad investment".
