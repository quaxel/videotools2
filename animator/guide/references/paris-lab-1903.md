Details that make it read as a 1900s French lab (pick 3-4 per 9:16 frame):
- Long wooden benches (dark oak or stained pine) on square legs, the top stained by spills, with racks of test tubes,
  retort stands with clamps, round and conical flasks, a spirit lamp or gas burner on a rubber hose.
- Open wall shelves of brown and clear reagent bottles with paper labels and glass stoppers, in rows.
- Plain plaster walls, often pale green-grey or cream, painted dado below; tall window with small panes and a white
  frame, a roller blind.
- Floors of small terracotta hexagonal tiles (tomettes, common in French interiors) or red quarry tiles: hard, so
  dropped glass shatters. This is our hook floor.
- Gas light: a wall bracket or a pendant with a glass shade; by 1903 also electric bulbs in some labs. Our night shots
  use an oil/gas lamp glow.
- His other trade (artist, designer): an easel with a canvas and a jar of brushes in a corner.

Palette: terracotta floor, sage-grey walls, dark oak, amber and clear glass; by lamplight gold; at night violet.
Lights strong enough to light the puppets: the tall window by day (cool distant light), the lamp on the bench at
night (warm point light).

The Paris street (about 1905) and the First World War trench in this video have no reference notes: built from general

duckboards, barbed-wire posts).
