# Research: the Ghost Army (23rd Headquarters Special Troops)

The Ghost Army was a secret US Army unit, the 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, that fought in France, Belgium,
Luxembourg and Germany in 1944-45 without real tanks or guns. Its camouflage engineers (many of them recruited
artists) inflated rubber dummy tanks, trucks and artillery with gas-powered air compressors and set them out where
enemy reconnaissance could see them. Sound teams drove half-tracks carrying 500-pound speakers that played
prerecorded troop movements and bridge building, audible up to 15 miles away, and signal operators ran "spoof radio"
— bogus traffic nets that imitated real units' operators. Together they let 1,100 men pass for two divisions,
about 30,000 men, so the enemy watched the fake army while the real units moved elsewhere. Its biggest operation,
Viersen (March 1945), used about 600 inflatable dummies to draw German attention away from the Ninth Army's real
Rhine crossing.

- Its 1,100 members could project the strength of two divisions, approximately 30,000 (thirty thousand) men — https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/ghost-army-canvas-and-camouflage

- "Spoof radio" generated bogus radio traffic — https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/ghost-army-canvas-and-camouflage
- Operation Viersen used about 600 inflatable dummies to draw German attention away from the real Rhine crossing — https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/ghost-army-canvas-and-camouflage
