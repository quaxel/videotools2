## Description

A $49 printer feels like a steal. Then two ink cartridges cost $68. That's not bad luck. That's the business model.

Home printers work like razors and blades: the machine gets you in the door, and the money comes from the ink you keep buying. Even the cheapest ink runs about $13 an ounce, which is pricier, ounce for ounce, than fine champagne. Every cartridge carries a tiny chip, and in 2016 one firmware update made printers reject cheaper third-party cartridges overnight.

Then there's cleaning. Printers spray ink just to keep the print heads from drying out. Testers found some burned half their ink or more without printing anything, and in a few only 20 to 30% ever reached the paper. One printer company boss even called every printer sold "an investment."

What's the most you've ever paid for ink? 👇

(Ink use depends on the model and how often you print; the 2016 block was later partly reversed by an optional update.)
