## Title
One Wrong Turn Started World War I? What If the Driver Knew the Way 🚗 #Shorts

## Description
A driver took one wrong turn, and stopped right in front of an assassin.

Sarajevo, 28 June 1914. Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife Sophie ride through the city in an open car. That morning a bomb is thrown at them; it bounces off the folded-back top and explodes under the next car. The Archduke decides to visit the wounded in hospital, but nobody tells the drivers. At the Latin Bridge the cars in front turn onto the old route and his driver, Leopold Lojka, follows. Governor Oskar Potiorek shouts that it is the wrong way; as the driver shifts into reverse, the car stands still a few steps from nineteen-year-old Gavrilo Princip, who fires two shots. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbia and declared war one month later, on 28 July; Russia backed Serbia, Germany backed Austria, France stood with Russia, and within a week most of Europe was at war. The war killed an estimated 15 to 22 million people, and four empires fell: the Russian, German, Austro-Hungarian and Ottoman. Many historians think a war would have come sooner or later anyway, but not that day, and not from that spark.

Would the war have happened anyway? 👇
