# Research: The King's Sticks: a thief who catches himself

## Mechanism

This is a story, not a statistic: a classic South Asian folktale told about Birbal, the wise advisor at Emperor
Akbar's court. A theft has a closed list of suspects; each one is handed an identical stick and told the thief's
stick will grow overnight. The sticks are ordinary wood, so an innocent suspect has no reason to touch theirs. Only
the guilty one, believing the threat, trims his stick to stay "the same length" and so becomes the only one that is
shorter. The trap works on the thief's own guilty belief, not on any magic. Retellings vary the suspects (servants,
nine jewelry attendants, guards) and the growth (one, two or three inches, a finger's breadth); this video's version
(twelve guards, one inch, the Queen's necklace, a palace woodshed) is the user's scenario.

## Claims

- The tale is a traditional Indian story about Birbal, advisor to an emperor; in that telling Birbal hands nine attendants identical sticks, says the dishonest one's stick will grow two inches overnight, and the thief cuts two inches off hers — https://www.wbur.org/circleround/2020/01/07/the-nine-sticks-circle-nikesh-patel-maulik-pancholy
- Versions of "Birbal and the growing sticks" tell the same trick with a merchant's servants: the thief trims his stick in the night and is the only one with a shorter stick — https://storibuzz.in/birbal-and-the-growing-sticks/
