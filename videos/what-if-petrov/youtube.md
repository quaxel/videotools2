## Title
What If Petrov Had Reported the Launch? The 1983 False Alarm 🚀 #Shorts

## Description
In 1983 a computer told one Soviet officer that American missiles were coming. He decided the computer was wrong.

Just after midnight on 26 September 1983, in the Serpukhov-15 bunker near Moscow, the early-warning satellites reported one American missile launch, then five. Weeks earlier the Soviets had shot down Korean Air Lines Flight 007, and Moscow feared a US first strike. Lieutenant Colonel Stanislav Petrov's job was to pass the warning up the chain. He reasoned that a real first strike would be hundreds of missiles, not five, and ground radar could not confirm anything, so he reported a false alarm. The satellites had mistaken sunlight on high-altitude clouds for launches. Some historians say other layers of checks would have stopped a launch anyway; others say the leaders would have had only minutes to decide. Petrov got no reward and, by his own account, a reprimand over paperwork. The story only came out in 1998. He later said he was 50/50.

What would you have reported? 👇
