# Research: Why a Volcano Invented the Bicycle

## Mechanism

In April 1815 Mount Tambora on Sumbawa (today Indonesia, then the Dutch East Indies) erupted; its climax on
10 April was the most powerful eruption in recorded history. It threw sulphur dioxide into the stratosphere, where it
made a veil of sulphate aerosols that reflected sunlight, and the global climate cooled. In Europe 1816 became the
"Year Without a Summer": the coldest summer on record, heavy rain, failed harvests and sharp rises in food prices.
Oats, the fuel of horses, grew scarce; horses starved or were slaughtered for meat and hides. In Mannheim (Baden)
Karl Drais (born 29 April 1785, so 32 in June 1817) built a two-wheeled "Laufmaschine" (running machine, later the
draisine, dandy horse or hobby-horse): two spoked wooden wheels in line under a wooden beam with a small padded
seat, a front wheel steered by a handlebar, an armrest over the steering, and no pedals: the rider sat and pushed
off the ground with his feet, then glided. His first reported ride was on 12 June 1817, from Mannheim to the
Schwetzinger Relaishaus (a coaching inn) and back, in about an hour. In January 1818 he received a grand-ducal
privilege (a Baden patent). Drais' biographer Hans-Erhard Lessing argues, from circumstantial evidence, that the
horse deaths after the 1816 crop failure drove Drais to look for a horseless vehicle; this is a hypothesis, not
proven, so the script hedges it. Pedals came in France in the 1860s, when cranks were fixed to the front hub.
