# Research: Why gyms count on you not showing up

## Mechanism

Gyms sell a flat monthly fee, not visits. People sign up overconfident about how often they will go, then go
far less, and keep paying for months after they stop going before they cancel. A study of three US health
clubs (7,752 members over three years) found members on monthly contracts over $70 went about 4.3 times a
month, paying more than $17 per expected visit when a 10-visit pass cost $10 a visit; they forgo about $600 on
average, expect 9.5 visits a month but make about 4.2, and cancel more than two months after their last visit.
Budget chains push this further: low dues, very large membership bases per club (Planet Fitness: about 19.7
million members across 2,722 clubs, roughly 7,200 per club), a model that works only because members do not
all come at once.

## Claims

- 7,752 members of three US health clubs tracked over three years (thousands of members, three years) — https://eml.berkeley.edu/~ulrike/Papers/gym.pdf
- Members who choose a flat monthly fee of over $70 (seventy dollars) attend on average 4.3 times per month (about four); they pay more than $17 per expected visit (over seventeen dollars) though a 10-visit pass costs $10 per visit (ten-visit pass, ten dollars) — https://eml.berkeley.edu/~ulrike/Papers/gym.pdf
- On average, these users forgo savings of $600 (six hundred dollars) during their membership — https://eml.berkeley.edu/~ulrike/Papers/gym.pdf
- Users predict 9.50 monthly visits (about nine); actual monthly visits are 4.17 (about four) — https://eml.berkeley.edu/~ulrike/Papers/gym.pdf
- Interval between last attendance and termination: 2.31 full months (over two months) — https://eml.berkeley.edu/~ulrike/Papers/gym.pdf
- Planet Fitness ended 2024 with about 19.7 million members and 2,722 clubs, an average of roughly 7,200 members per club (around seven thousand, my division of the two totals) — https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1637207/000163720725000016/plnt-20241231.htm

Notes: the study data are from three clubs around 1997–2000; the per-club figure is an average across the chain,
not a capacity. The claim that the model "works only if members do not all come at once" is an inference, phrased
as such in the script.
