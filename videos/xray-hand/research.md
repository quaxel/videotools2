# Research: The Green Glow That Looked Inside the Human Body

## Mechanism

On 8 November 1895, in his darkened laboratory at the University of Würzburg, the physicist Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen
(born 27 March 1845, so 50) was running a cathode-ray tube of the Crookes-Hittorf kind from an induction coil. He had
covered the tube with black cardboard (his paper says black paper) so that none of its own glow could escape, yet a
screen painted with barium platinocyanide on a bench some distance away lit up with a green fluorescence. The rays
went through a book of a thousand pages, through thick blocks of wood, through cards and tinfoil; lead stopped them.
Held in front of the screen, a hand showed the shadow of its bones with only faint outlines of the flesh. The rays
come from the tube itself: the fast electrons of the cathode beam slam into the glass wall (or the anode) and give
off X-rays. Soft tissue lets them through; calcium in bone (and the metal of a ring) absorbs them, so bone and metal
leave a shadow on the glowing screen or a dark-light pattern on a photographic plate. He worked on the rays for about
seven weeks, on 22 December 1895 made a photographic plate of his wife Anna Bertha's hand with her wedding ring, and
on 28 December submitted "On a New Kind of Rays". He called them X-rays because X stands for the unknown. The news
went round the world within weeks; he won the first Nobel Prize in Physics in 1901 and never patented the rays.

## Costume references (photos looked at on Wikimedia Commons, 2026-11-10)

- Röntgen portraits of the 1890s (Commons media search "Wilhelm Röntgen portrait", six studio portraits): thick dark
  hair brushed up and back from a high forehead, long full dark beard (later portraits grey), dark frock coat or
  jacket buttoned high over a dark waistcoat, white stand-up collar, dark tie. No lab coat in any of them.
- Anna Bertha Röntgen portrait (Commons search "Anna Bertha Röntgen"): hair parted in the middle and pinned up,
  high-necked dark dress with long sleeves and a small brooch.
- The 1895 plate "Hand mit Ringen": the ring sits on the ring finger as a dark band at the first knuckle.
- The Würzburg laboratory memorial room (Commons search "Röntgen laboratory"): pale walls, tall windows with heavy dark
  curtains, wooden work tables, a glass-fronted instrument cabinet, a pendant lamp, parquet floor.
