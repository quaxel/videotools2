## Title
The Most Famous Computer Bug Was a Real Moth 🦋💻 #Shorts

## Description
The most famous computer bug in history was an actual bug, trapped inside a giant machine.

Harvard, 9 September 1947: the Mark II, a giant calculator built from electromagnetic relays, was being tested when its operators traced an error to Panel F, Relay #70. Between the relay's contact points sat a moth. They taped it into the logbook at the 1545 entry and wrote: "First actual case of bug being found." The joke is the word "actual": a relay only works when its two contacts touch, and engineers had already called hidden faults "bugs" for decades. Thomas Edison wrote about them in 1878. Grace Hopper, who worked on the Mark II, told the story again and again, and the logbook page, moth and all, is kept today by the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History.

Did you know "bug" was older than computers? 👇
