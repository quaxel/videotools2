## Title
In Total Darkness, a Green Screen Showed the Bones of a Living Hand 💀 #Shorts

## Description
In a pitch-black lab, a screen glowed green and showed the bones inside a living hand.

On November 8, 1895, in Würzburg, physicist Wilhelm Röntgen covered a cathode-ray tube in black cardboard and darkened his lab, yet a screen painted with barium platinocyanide across the room lit up. A thousand-page book and a block of wood could not stop the invisible rays, and a hand held in front of the screen showed its bones. After about seven weeks of tests, on December 22, 1895, he photographed his wife Anna Bertha's hand with her wedding ring; she reportedly said "I have seen my death." The rays come from fast electrons hitting the glass of the tube; skin lets them through, bone and metal block them. He called them X-rays, X for unknown, sent off his paper on December 28, 1895, won the first Nobel Prize in Physics in 1901 and never patented the rays.

Would you have been brave enough to put your hand in front of that screen? 👇
