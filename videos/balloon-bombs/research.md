# Research: The WWII Balloon Bombs That Reached America

## Mechanism

Between November 1944 and April 1945 the Japanese Army launched about 9,300 Fu-Go balloons from beaches on the
Pacific coast of Honshu (Ichinomiya, Otsu, Nakoso). Each was a hydrogen-filled envelope 33 ft (10 m) across, made
of washi paper laminated and glued by thousands of mobilised teenage schoolgirls, carrying four incendiaries and an
anti-personnel bomb. At 30,000-35,000 ft the winter jet stream, with winds approaching 200 mph, carried them at least
6,000 miles to North America in 30-100 hours; an altimeter-driven ring dropped sandbags whenever the balloon sank.
About 300 were found or observed, in 19 US states and territories (Alaska, Hawaii, Montana, Iowa, Texas, as far east
as Farmington, Michigan), Canada and Mexico. US officials asked the press to publish nothing, which baffled the
Japanese; the campaign was halted by mid-April 1945. USGS geologists studied the sand in the ballast bags to locate
the launch beaches (they named Ichinomiya as a candidate, which was correct). On 5 May 1945 near Bly, Oregon, Elsie
Mitchell and five children from her Sunday school picnic found a balloon in the woods; it exploded and killed all
six, the only deaths by enemy action in the contiguous United States in the war.

## Claims

- Japan launched about nine thousand three hundred (9,300) balloons, November 1944 to April 1945 — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu-Go_balloon_bomb
- The envelopes were washi paper glued by thousands of teenage schoolgirls — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu-Go_balloon_bomb
- Hydrogen-filled; flew at thirty-five thousand (30,000-35,000) feet in the jet stream — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu-Go_balloon_bomb
- The launch sites were at least six thousand (6,000) miles from the US mainland — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu-Go_balloon_bomb
- Ballast sandbags were dropped when the balloon sank (altimeter control) — https://www.globalsecurity.org/military/world/japan/fu-go-jp.htm
- About three hundred (300) were found or observed in North America, including Alaska, Montana and Michigan — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu-Go_balloon_bomb
- US officials asked the press to publish nothing; the silence baffled the Japanese — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu-Go_balloon_bomb
- USGS geologists studied the ballast sand to find the launch beaches — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu-Go_balloon_bomb
- On May fifth, nineteen forty-five (5 May 1945), a church picnic near Bly, Oregon found a balloon bomb; six (6) died, the only deaths by enemy action on the US mainland — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu-Go_balloon_bomb
- Mitchell Monument marks the site in a ponderosa pine forest — https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/fremont-winema/recreation/mitchell-monument-documentary-site
