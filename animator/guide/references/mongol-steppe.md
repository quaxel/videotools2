# Mongolian steppe, gers and a yam post station

Sources looked at (Wikimedia Commons, 2026-11-06): media search "Mongolian steppe yurt" (gers alone on open grass under
big skies, low bare hills behind; dusk shots of a ger camp in violet light), "paiza Mongol" (museum photos of the
Metropolitan Museum iron and silver paiza and others: tall rounded tablets with a ring at the top and vertical lines of
script; one round type with a beast's head), and Polo's own description of the post houses (Yule ed., Book 2 ch. 26:
large buildings, horses standing ready saddled, a clerk who notes arrival times).

## What makes it read

1. Flat, wide short grass to the horizon, tan and olive patches, no trees. Ridges are low, round and bare, fading
   blue-violet in layers.
2. The sky is most of the frame: deep blue with long streaks of cloud by day, violet and pink at dusk.
3. Ger: a white felt drum (about 2 m tall walls 1.4 m, diameter 4-5 m) with a low cone roof, two or three ropes
   bound round the wall and roof, a small stovepipe at the crown, and a bright orange-red painted wooden door with a
   carved frame. Sits straight on the grass.
4. Post station (our reading of Polo): a long timber building with a low tiled roof and a banner, a hitching rail with
   saddled horses standing ready, more horses grazing loose behind, a mile post by the road.
5. Riders: long deel coats belted with a sash, fur-trimmed hats; Polo's express riders bound with bands round chest
   and head and a belt hung with bells.
6. Paiza: a tall rounded metal tablet with a ring at the top, dark iron with silver lines; we draw a falcon on it
   (Polo's "gerfalcon tablet") and keep it 2x real size.

## Lights strong enough for puppets

Low sun at dawn and dusk (warm side light, distant), the station lantern at night (point light, amber), torches.
