# Research: Why a city poured 96 million black balls into its reservoir

Idea: ideation DB idea-006 (doc-idea-006). Limitations respected: the video does not present the balls as only an
evaporation trick (it says evaporation is a bonus and the main reason was sunlight forming bromate), and it leaves out
the separate debate about the water used to manufacture the balls.

## Mechanism

In 2007 the city's water utility found bromate above the federal (EPA) limit in some open reservoirs. Its studies
concluded that water containing bromide and chlorine formed bromate in the presence of sunlight: bottles hung at
different depths in a reservoir showed that samples nearer the surface, with more sunlight, formed more bromate, and
bromate did not form when light transmission was cut by 80-85 %. Floating covers were estimated at over $300 million;
shade balls (4-inch black high-density polyethylene, 36 cents each) block about 95 % of sunlight. They are black
because carbon black is added as a UV stabiliser that extends their life. They also cut evaporation (85-90 %). The
largest deployment, at Los Angeles Reservoir, reached 96 million balls in August 2015 (one utility FAQ says 98 million;
the script uses the 96 million confirmed by press coverage at the time).
