## Title
What If the Dinosaur Asteroid Had Hit Somewhere Else? 🦖☄️ #Shorts

## Description
The asteroid that killed the giant dinosaurs may have hit one of the unluckiest spots on Earth.

About 66 million years ago a rock roughly six miles (10 km) wide or more slammed into a shallow sea in what is now Mexico, on rock packed with sulfur and hydrocarbons. The blast threw dust and sulfur into the stratosphere; Kunio Kaiho and Naga Oshima argue that soot from the burning hydrocarbons darkened the sky too. The planet cooled, and about 75% of plant and animal species vanished, including all the non-avian dinosaurs (birds survived). In their 2017 Scientific Reports study, the two Japanese scientists mapped hydrocarbon-rich rock worldwide: it covers only about 13% of Earth's surface. Anywhere in the other 87%, their model gives far milder cooling, and the dinosaurs might have survived. Other scientists, such as Sean Gulick, question those assumptions and argue the sulfur alone was enough. Meanwhile the largest mammals right after the impact were no bigger than rats, and with the giants gone, mammals took over.

Do you think the dinosaurs would have made it? 👇
