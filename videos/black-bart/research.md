# Black Bart: the gentleman bandit caught by his laundry

Charles E. Boles robbed stagecoaches in the gold country of northern California at least 28 times, a sack over his
head, a shotgun in his hands that he reportedly never fired. Nobody saw his face. In 1883 a young man along the road
shot at him as he ran from a holdup; Boles was hit lightly and fled, and the posse found a handkerchief at the scene
carrying a laundry mark, F.X.O.7. City laundries stamped a small code on every customer's linen so it came back to
the right owner, and each laundry kept the codes in its own book. The detectives James Hume and Harry Morse took the
mark from laundry to laundry in San Francisco until one matched it to a regular customer, "Charles Bolton", a
well-dressed gentleman who said he was in mining. The bandit nobody had seen was found through his washing.

## Left out

- How many laundries the detectives checked: about 90 (Wikipedia) vs 91 (Mental Floss). The script says "laundry after laundry".
- Whether the handkerchief was found at the last robbery or wrapped round buckshot at an earlier one: HistoryNet places it differently from Wikipedia. The script only says it was found after the robbery where he was shot.
- The "Po8" signature: one source only.
- The six-year San Quentin sentence, his release in 1888 and his disappearance: one source, and they are after the ending.
- The derby, eyeglasses, binoculars, food left at the scene: the sources list different items.
- The stage company's name: a brand; the script says "stagecoaches" and "the strongbox".
- "Never loaded his shotgun": a "may have" in one source.
