# Research: Why traffic jams form with no obstacle ahead

Idea: ideation DB idea-005 (doc-idea-005). Limitation respected: not every jam forms this way; bottlenecks
(on-ramps, tunnels, sags) still cause jams — the paper says they do it by pushing density over the critical value.
The video says so explicitly and presents the experiment as one mechanism.

## Mechanism

Sugiyama et al. (New Journal of Physics, 2008) put 22 vehicles on a circular road 230 m round, with no bottleneck,
asked the drivers to cruise at about 30 km/h and to follow the car ahead safely. The free flow held for a while,
then small fluctuations in the gaps between cars grew; several vehicles were forced to stop completely for a moment
and a stop-and-go wave appeared (a snapshot 3 minutes later shows a jam of five vehicles). Cars outside the
cluster moved at about 40 km/h; cars left the front of the jam while others joined its back, so the jam cluster
itself travelled backward at roughly 20 km/h, the same value measured on real highways. A jam forms spontaneously
when average density exceeds a critical value; bottlenecks matter because they raise density over it.
