# The day Niagara Falls went silent (1848)

After a hard winter, Lake Erie lay under thick ice. In late March 1848 a warm spell broke it into floes, and two days
of gale-force winds pushed the ice fields across the lake to its outlet. The current carried the floes into the
mouth of the Niagara River between Buffalo and Fort Erie, where they jammed into a dam that choked the river at its
source. Downstream the water drained away: on the night of March 29 to March 30 the flow over the Horseshoe and
American Falls dropped to a trickle and people near the falls woke to silence, no roar and no mist. Mill workers
found their water wheels stopped. For about thirty hours people walked, and some rode horses, over the exposed rock
floor of the channel; some carried torches out toward the brink, and relics of the War of 1812 were picked up from
the bed. Some feared the end of the world and churches held services. Then the wind shifted, the ice dam broke, and a
wall of water came down the channel and over the brink with a roar.
