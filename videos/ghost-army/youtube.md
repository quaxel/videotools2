## Description
These World War Two tanks were full of air, and that was the whole point.

The 23rd Headquarters Special Troops, nicknamed the Ghost Army, was a secret US unit of about 1,100 men, many of them artists. In France, Belgium, Luxembourg and Germany in 1944-45 they inflated rubber tanks, trucks and guns with air compressors and set them out where enemy scouts could see them. Half-tracks carrying 500-pound speakers played recorded troop movements, audible up to 15 miles away, and radio operators sent fake messages. Together they could pass for two divisions, about 30,000 men. While the enemy watched the fake army, the real troops moved somewhere else. The unit's records stayed classified until 1996.
