## The fort (Periods III-IV, c. 97-105+)
1. **Almost all timber, turf and wattle-and-daub**; stone only from c. 160. No Hadrian's Wall in frame.
2. **Turf rampart** (~4.5 m wide) with a **timber gateway**: oak posts in pairs, alder planks, a lintel.
3. **Roads**: clay topped with stone chippings; straight, crossing at right angles; puddles, wheel ruts.
4. **Barracks**: long rows of single-room units, two rows back to back, a **verandah** (posts and a lean-to
   roof) along the front; bigger centurion's quarters at the end. Walls: oak frame, wattle and daub, plaster wash
   (pale, off-white to ochre). Roofs: shingles or thatch (we use dark grey-brown shingles, repeating rhythm).
5. Floors of dirt or timber with **bracken strewn** on them; ovens, a forge, a wooden water pipe (alder trunks).

## The bathhouse (pre-Hadrianic, outside the fort, SE of the gate, on a slope above a stream)
1. **Rooms in a row** (Reihentyp): cold rooms at one end, hot rooms with **hypocaust** and the **furnace** at the
   other; a cold plunge pool on one side. Brick and tile made on site.
2. **Hypocaust**: raised floor on stacks of square tiles (pilae); hot gas from the furnace runs under it and up
   through flue tiles in the walls. Our cutaway shows this.
3. **Plunge baths** with steps down and a **pink waterproof lining** (crushed-tile mortar).
4. Plastered walls in pale warm tones, tile-red details, framed or arched openings into the next room, wet floors.

## Palette

## Light strong enough for the puppets
Outside: the low sun. In the bathhouse: warm light from high windows and the furnace mouth (strong, orange).
