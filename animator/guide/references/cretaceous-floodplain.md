# Late Cretaceous floodplain (Hell Creek type), 66 million years ago

Set: `CretaceousSet`, in a video's own folder (find it with `grep -rln CretaceousSet src/videos` from `animator/`).

Sources (read, nothing downloaded):
- Hell Creek Formation overview — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell_Creek_Formation
- Hell Creek megaflora, SW North Dakota (angiosperms ~90 %, the rest conifers, cycadophytes, ferns) — https://archives.datapages.com/data/ndgs/data/011/021/021.html
- Hell Creek palynology, Montana (conifer forest with an undergrowth of ferns and flowering plants) — https://gsa.confex.com/gsa/2004AM/webprogram/Paper73636.html
- Denver Museum's Hell Creek forest reconstruction (via the Hell Creek article) and Carleton's T. rex pages — https://hoopermuseum.earthsci.carleton.ca/rex/p4e.htm

Details that make it read as "dinosaur time" in a narrow 9:16 frame:
1. A flat, warm, humid floodplain on a coastal plain: a lazy river / swampy pools, sandbars.
2. Tall conifers (araucaria / redwood-like) as the skyline: straight trunks, tiers of flat dark-green crowns.
3. Undergrowth of big ferns (fronds radiating from a crown) and cycads (a stubby trunk with a palm-like rosette).
4. Broad-leaved flowering shrubs (magnolia-like) with a few pale flowers; low palmettos near water.
5. Distant hazy hills and a volcano-free sky (we keep the sky plain blue-gold: the volcanoes cliché is not this site).
6. No grass meadows: grasses were not yet widespread; ground is mud, leaf litter, horsetails and low ferns.

Palette: warm greens (fern #5f9a48, conifer #2f5a3a), ochre mud (#b8925a), river teal (#4aa0a8), sky blue to warm
gold at the horizon. After the impact: the same set re-lit — a grey-brown dusty sky with a dim red sun, then cold
blue-grey with frost and falling ash/snow, plants drooping brown.

Light strong enough for puppets/models: the low warm sun (a distant light). In the dark world, nothing strong: dim
cold ambient only, and the impact flash (white) from the sky.
