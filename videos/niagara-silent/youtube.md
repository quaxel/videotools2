## Title
The Day Niagara Falls Went Silent 🤫 | March 1848 #Shorts

## Description
Niagara Falls once went completely silent.

In late March 1848, after a hard winter, strong winds pushed huge fields of broken ice across Lake Erie. The ice jammed into the mouth of the Niagara River between Buffalo and Fort Erie and plugged it like a cork. Downstream the water drained away, and people near the falls woke to an eerie silence: no roar and no mist. Mill wheels stopped. For about 30 hours people walked across the bare rock of the riverbed, some on horseback, and relics from the War of 1812 turned up on the rocks. Then the wind shifted, the ice broke, and a wall of water came roaring back over the falls.
