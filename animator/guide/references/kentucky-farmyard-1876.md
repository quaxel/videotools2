## What makes it read as this place

The yard
- A worm fence: 6-8 split rails stacked in a zigzag, weathered silver-grey with dark ends, crossed stake pairs at
  some corners. The 1876 report says the meat stuck to the fences — a good spot to show pieces landing.
- A low log house: hewn horizontal logs (warm brown) with pale cream chinking stripes between them, a stone end
  chimney with a thin smoke line, a wood-shingle roof, a small multi-pane window, a plank door. A dogtrot breezeway
  between two pens is optional (reads well in a wide shot).
- Short early-March grass, still partly tan and green; bare dirt where people walk; a woodpile.

The soap station (Mrs. Crouch's work)
- A big round black cast-iron kettle on three short legs over a log fire, steam rising from it.
- A long wooden stirring paddle.
- A V-shaped wooden ash hopper on a frame, dripping brown lye into a wooden bucket; a lye barrel; a fat bucket.

Beyond
- Rolling pasture, a line of bare early-spring trees, and wooded knobs fading blue behind (Ridge + Haze with parallax).
- Sky: clear, deep blue at the top to pale near the horizon. **No clouds, no storm** — this is the story.

## Palette

Clear blue sky (#5d9be0 → #cfe4f6), weathered rail grey (#9a958a, shade #6c675e), log brown (#8a5e3c) with cream
chinking (#e6d9bc), fieldstone grey, March grass (#8fa85a / tan #b8a06a), black iron kettle, orange fire, soft blue knobs
(#7c93a8). The meat pieces: small pink-red flakes (#c8505a with lighter #e89090 edge) — they must stand out on grass.

## Strong lights

A bright late-morning sun (warm-white distant light from high left) is the key light. The soap fire under the kettle
is small but close: add a `firePoint` when Mrs. Crouch stands at the kettle so her near side flickers warm. Nothing else.

## In the narrow 9:16 frame

Kettle + Mrs. Crouch + one fence run + log-house corner with chimney. Wide shot: fence zigzag leading the eye to the
house under a big empty sky (lots of sky headroom for falling flakes and, later, distant vulture silhouettes).
