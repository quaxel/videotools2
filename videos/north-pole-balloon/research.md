# Research: The North Pole Expedition Lost for 33 Years (Andrée, 1897)

## Mechanism

On 11 July 1897 the Swede S. A. Andrée, Nils Strindberg and Knut Frænkel lifted off from a plank balloon hangar on
Danes Island (Danskøya), Svalbard, in the hydrogen balloon Örnen ("the Eagle"), a 20.5 m envelope of varnished
three-layer silk with a small basket. Andrée meant to steer with long drag ropes trailing on the ice, but right after
takeoff about 530 kg of rope came loose and fell away, so the balloon flew too high, lost hydrogen and could not be
steered. Rain and fog then froze on the envelope and weighed it down; after 65 hours, much of it bumping along the
ice, they came down on the pack ice on 14 July at 82°56′ N, far short of the pole. They walked and hauled sledges
over the ice for about three months (400 km), drifting with the floe, and in early October 1897 reached Kvitøya
(White Island), where all three died. Nothing was heard of them until 5 August 1930, when the Norwegian sealer
Bratvaag (Gunnar Horn's expedition) landed on Kvitøya and found their boat, the remains, Andrée's diary and Frænkel's
and Strindberg's journals; Strindberg's exposed film was found too, and 93 of its 240 frames were saved and developed.

## Claims

- On 11 July eighteen ninety-seven (1897) three men, Andrée, Strindberg and Frænkel, flew the hydrogen balloon Örnen ("the Eagle") from Danes Island toward the North Pole — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9e%27s_Arctic_balloon_expedition
- The envelope was varnished three-layer silk, 20.5 m across, launched from a plank hangar; it had no engine and was meant to be steered by drag ropes — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9e%27s_Arctic_balloon_expedition
- Right after takeoff the drag ropes (about 530 kg) fell away — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9e%27s_Arctic_balloon_expedition
- Rain and fog caused ice to form on the balloon; after sixty-five (65) hours aloft they were forced to land — https://svalbardmuseum.no/en/the-andre-expedition
- They came down on the pack ice on 14 July 1897 at 82°56′ N, far short of the pole — https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/polar-record/article/abs/could-the-members-of-the-1897-andree-balloon-expedition-have-survived/E1702FD895FA784D178F3DDEA5D81794
- They trekked across the ice for three (3) months, about 400 km, hauling sledges of over 200 kg — https://svalbardmuseum.no/en/the-andre-expedition ; https://oceanwide-expeditions.com/blog/the-flight-of-the-ornen-andree-s-1897-arctic-balloon-expedition
- They reached Kvitøya (White Island) in early October 1897 and all three died there — https://svalbardmuseum.no/en/the-andre-expedition
- Thirty-three (33) years later, in 1930, seal hunters of the Norwegian sealer Bratvaag found their boat, remains and diaries on the island — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9e%27s_Arctic_balloon_expedition ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratvaag_Expedition
- Strindberg's film was found and could still be developed; ninety-three (93) of 240 frames were saved — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9e%27s_Arctic_balloon_expedition
- The three men (3) were buried from Storkyrkan, Stockholm, on 5 October 1930 — https://svalbardmuseum.no/en/the-andre-expedition
