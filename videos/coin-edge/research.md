# Research — The Coin That Wrote a Warning on Its Edge

Before the 1660s English silver coins were struck by hand with a hammer, so their edges were rough and uneven.
Clippers shaved slivers of silver off the rim, melted the clippings down, and spent the lighter coins at full face
value; nobody could tell from the ragged edge. From the 1660s the Mint struck coins by machine (the "milled"
coinage) and Pierre Blondeau's process stamped lettering onto the edge of the large coins: DECUS ET TUTAMEN,
"an ornament and a safeguard". A clipped edge no longer looks rough, it loses letters, so the theft shows.
By the 1690s the old hammered silver was so badly clipped that the government called it in: the Great Recoinage
of 1696, run by the Royal Mint, then housed inside the Tower of London. Isaac Newton became Warden of the Mint in
1696; his duty was to hunt down clippers and counterfeiters, and he interviewed criminals and informers himself.
The recoinage was finished within three years.

The scene where a clipper is caught by the cut lettering on a new coin is a dramatisation of why the edge works,
not a documented arrest: clippers mostly left the lettered milled coins alone, which is exactly the point.
Newton did not invent the edge lettering (Blondeau did, decades earlier).

## Claims

- Old hand-struck silver coins were "clipped around the edges"; post-1662 machine-struck coins were "protected from clipping by an engraved, decorated and milled edge" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recoinage_of_1696
- The recoinage of sixteen ninety-six replaced the old silver coins — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recoinage_of_1696
- Decus et tutamen means "an ornament and a safeguard"; the edge inscription protected against clipping — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decus_et_tutamen
- Pierre Blondeau "pioneered the process of stamping lettering onto the edge of coins" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Blondeau
- "The Royal Mint was then in the Tower of London." — https://www.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/journal/people/isaac-newton/
- Isaac Newton was Warden and Master of the Royal Mint from sixteen ninety-six; his duty was "investigating and bringing to justice those who clipped and counterfeited the coin of the realm" — https://www.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/journal/people/isaac-newton/
- Newton "is known to have himself conducted interviews with criminals and informers." — https://www.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/journal/people/isaac-newton/
- The recoinage "was completed within three years." — https://www.royalmintmuseum.org.uk/journal/people/isaac-newton/
