# Research: Why airports make you walk farther

## Mechanism

Waiting feels longer when there is nothing to do. David Maister's 1985 paper "The Psychology of Waiting Lines" opens
with the rule "Occupied time feels shorter than unoccupied time", and gives the example of a hotel whose complaints
about slow elevators fell after it put mirrors next to them, though the wait did not change. The best-known case is a
Houston airport (told in the New York Times in 2012): passengers complained about waiting for bags; more baggage
handlers cut the average wait to eight minutes, but complaints went on. The walk from the gate took one minute and the
rest was seven minutes standing at the carousel. The airport moved arrival gates away from the main terminal and sent
bags to the outermost carousel, so passengers walked six times longer, and complaints dropped to near zero.
