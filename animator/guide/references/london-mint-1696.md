## What makes it read as this place

- **The Tower skyline:** a pale, whitened square keep, wider than tall, with four corner turrets topped by small
  lead cupolas, crenellated walls in front; seen at night through a garret window it is the one silhouette that
  says "London, the Mint".
- **The coining press:** an iron screw press in a heavy frame, a long horizontal bar (the fly) with a big iron ball
  at each end, swung by hand; the coin blank sits between two dies under the ram.
- **The coins:** hammered coins are thin, not quite round, the rim ragged and lettering half off the flan;
  the machine-struck crown (about 38 mm) is perfectly round with a raised rim and lettering round its edge.
- **The market street:** brick shopfronts rebuilt after the Great Fire, painted shop signs hanging out on iron
  brackets, a cobbled street, stalls with awnings, a town crier with a hand bell.
- **The garret:** low beams, a guttering candle in a pewter stick, a crucible over a small brazier.

## Palette

Pale limestone white and grey for the Tower, black iron, warm red brick, cobble grey, silver with cool highlights,
candle amber, furnace orange.

## Strong lights

The candle in the garret, the melting brazier, the Mint's furnace glow (orange, from low and to the side), daylight
in the street and the bakery's window. Night in Newton's room: one candle on the desk.
