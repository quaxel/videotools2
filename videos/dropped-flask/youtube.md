## Title
The Dropped Flask That Explains Your Windshield 🧪 #Shorts

## Description
A glass flask shattered on a hard floor, and then it simply refused to fall apart.

Paris, 1903: the chemist and artist Édouard Bénédictus knocked a glass flask to the floor of his lab. It cracked into a web but kept its shape, because it had once held collodion, a liquid plastic that dried into a thin clear film inside and held every shard. The story goes that he later read about a car crash where people were cut by flying glass, remembered the flask and worked through the night: glass, a thin layer of plastic, then glass again. He patented it in 1909, but car makers were slow to want it, so its first big job was the eyepieces of gas masks in the First World War. Then it went into car windshields, where laminated glass is still the standard today: when it breaks, the pieces stick to the plastic layer.
