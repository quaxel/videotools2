## Description
He robbed at least 28 stagecoaches, and the thing that finally caught him was his laundry.

Black Bart held up stagecoaches across northern California with a sack over his head and a shotgun he reportedly never fired, and he left poems behind. Nobody saw his face. In 1883 a young man fired at him as he ran from a holdup; he was only lightly wounded, but he left a handkerchief behind carrying a laundry mark: F.X.O.7. City laundries marked their customers' linen with codes, so detectives James Hume and Harry Morse took the mark from laundry to laundry until one recognised it. It belonged to a customer called Charles Bolton, a gentleman who said he was in mining. Charles Bolton was Black Bart.
