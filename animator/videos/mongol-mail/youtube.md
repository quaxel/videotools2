## Title
The Mongol Mail That Never Stopped 🐎 Marco Polo's 200 Miles a Day #Shorts

## Description
Marco Polo wrote that the Khan's letters raced 200 miles in a day, yet no single horse made the trip.

The Mongol empire ran a horse relay called the yam, founded by Ogedei Khan. Polo described it in Kublai Khan's China: a post station at every 25 miles of road, some keeping 400 horses, 200 saddled at the station and 200 out at grass, and by his count more than 10,000 stations and 300,000 horses. The villages near each station had to supply the horses. Express riders bound their stomach, chest and head with strong bands, wore belts hung with bells and carried a tablet, the paiza, that let them take any horse they met on the road. When the next station heard the bells, it had a fresh horse and rider ready, and a clerk noted the time. So the letter changed hands at full gallop: 200 to 250 miles by day, Polo says, and as much again by night. Fruit picked one morning in the capital reached the Khan the next evening, ten days' journey away.

Would you have handed your horse to a man with a falcon tablet? 👇
