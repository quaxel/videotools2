## Description

You've jabbed "close door" six times and the doors haven't moved. That's not a broken button. In many elevators, it was never meant to work for you.

Since the Americans with Disabilities Act passed in 1990, elevator doors have to stay open long enough for wheelchairs and crutches: at least 3 seconds, and pressing "close door" can't cut that short. So in many elevators the button does basically nothing in everyday use. It only comes alive in fire service mode, when a firefighter turns a key and needs full control.

Crosswalks pull the same trick. In 2004, New York found that more than 2,500 of its 3,250 walk buttons did nothing. They were leftovers from the 1970s, before computers ran the lights, and removing them would have cost about $1 million.

How many times do you press "close door"? 👇

(Not every close button is dead: some work after the minimum hold time, and older elevators may close at once.)
