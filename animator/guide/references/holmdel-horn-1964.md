# The horn antenna on Crawford Hill, Holmdel, New Jersey, 1964 (pigeon-static)

Sources looked at (Wikimedia Commons category of the antenna, 2026-11-10; file titles not quoted because they carry the
owning company's name, which the video leaves out):
- A 1962 black-and-white press photo (public domain, NASA archive): the whole antenna side-on, two men in white shirts
  standing on its platform (the caption does not name them).
- A colour photo from the mouth side at winter dusk, a stone landmark marker in front.
- A colour summer photo from the cab side: trees behind, gravel and grass, the stair up to the cab.
- A colour photo looking up into the horn from below: the dark inside, the steel truss underneath.
- The English Wikipedia article on the antenna for the numbers: 50 ft long, 20 x 20 ft aperture, aluminium horn,
  30 ft elevation wheel on rollers, the base frame turning on a 30 ft circular track, the cab at the apex.

What the photos show (and what reads in a 9:16 frame):
- The horn is a long four-sided pyramid of flat aluminium panels, grey-silver, laid on its side: narrow at the cab end,
  widening to a huge open end. Its outside is a grid of riveted panels with diagonal truss bracing.
- The big end is not a flat square: the top panel curves over like a scoop (the reflector), and the opening faces
  sideways, a dark square mouth about as tall as three people.
- A big spoked wheel (the elevation wheel, like a bicycle wheel taller than the horn is wide) rings the horn near its
  middle and rides on rollers on the base frame.
- The narrow end runs into a small box cab of vertical white boards with one square window, up on a steel stilt frame
  with a stair and a railing.
- Everything stands on a low flat steel platform (triangular base frame) on a circular rail track on gravel; railings,
  a stair, a small equipment shed on the platform.
- Setting: a hilltop clearing, low trees all round, gravel and rough grass.

Palette: weathered aluminium grey with a blue cast in shade and pale warm in sun, white cab boards, rust-red and dark
grey steel frame, green trees, gravel beige. Inside: dark grey-green metal walls converging to the small throat.

Lights strong enough to light the puppets: the sun (day shots), the cab window and a work lamp at night, a flashlight
inside the horn. The receiver racks inside the cab glow faintly (meter faces, the chart recorder).

Pick for the narrow frame: the dark mouth with its curved lip, the spoked wheel crossing the horn, the panel grid with
diagonal braces, the white cab on stilts with its window and stair.

Drawn smaller than real: about 0.75 of real size against the 1.3 m puppets (an 11 m horn, a 4.5 m mouth), so the cab
and the platform stay near the people.
