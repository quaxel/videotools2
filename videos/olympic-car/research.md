# Research: The Olympic Marathon Runner Who Took a Car

## Mechanism

The 1904 Olympic marathon in St. Louis started at 3 pm on 30 August in heat of about 90-92°F, on dusty, hilly
country roads with officials' cars raising dust ahead of the runners and only one real water source (a well near
the halfway point). American Fred Lorz dropped out with cramps after about nine miles and rode back toward the
stadium in a car (his manager's, by some accounts) for about eleven miles. When the car broke down around mile 19,
he got out and ran the rest, entering the stadium first to cheers; he was hailed as the winner and photographed with
Alice Roosevelt. Spectators and officials learned about the ride, he admitted it (calling it a joke), and he was
disqualified (the AAU banned him, later reduced). The official winner, Thomas Hicks, finished in 3:28:53 so exhausted
that his support team held him up and helped him across the line. Sources differ on details (the length of the ride,
whether it ended because the car broke down); the script uses the widely reported version.
