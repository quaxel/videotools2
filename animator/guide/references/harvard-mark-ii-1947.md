# Harvard Computation Laboratory and the Mark II relay calculator, 1947 (relay-moth)

## Sources looked at

- The logbook page, photo NH 96566-KN (US Naval History and Heritage Command), on Wikimedia Commons:
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:First_Computer_Bug,_1947.jpg
- Harvard Mark I photos on Wikimedia Commons (the same lab's earlier machine, the house style of Aiken's machines):
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Harvard_Mark_I_Computer_-_Left_Segment.jpg and the "Harvard Mark II" media search
  (glass-fronted black panels, rows of relays behind glass, switch banks, a paper-tape unit)
- Harvard Mark II article (built from electromagnetic relays, two sub-computers, a room of panels):
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Mark_II
- Relay anatomy (coil, armature, contact springs): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relay
- No clear photo of the Mark II's own racks was found on Commons; the racks are built from the Mark I look and the
  standard telephone-type relay of the 1940s.

## Details that make it read

1. Tall steel racks (about 2 m) in long rows, painted dark grey-green, each panel a grid of small open relays in
   horizontal rows, wires laced behind them.
2. A 1940s telephone-type relay: a cylindrical wound coil (dark red-brown or black insulation) on an L-shaped
   heel piece, a flat armature hinged at the back, and a stack of thin leaf springs at the front, each with a small
   silver contact point; the armature lifts the springs so two points touch.
3. A small number plate under every relay (68, 69, 70 ...) and a letter plate at the top of each panel (PANEL F).
4. The end panel of a row carries the machine's name plate (MARK II).
5. Operators' desk in front: a printing typewriter for the output, a paper-tape reader, a row of indicator lamps.
6. The logbook: a bound notebook of squared (grid) paper, entries in pencil and ink with times in the left margin
   (0800, 1000, 1525, 1545, 1700), the moth taped flat with a strip of clear tape gone amber.
7. The room: plain plaster walls in institutional cream and pale green, a wood or linoleum floor, tall windows,
   hanging enamel lamps.
