# Research: What if Archduke Franz Ferdinand's driver had known the route? (Sarajevo, 28 June 1914)

## Mechanism

On 28 June 1914 Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife Sophie rode through Sarajevo in a Gräf & Stift open car with the top folded down. That morning Nedeljko Čabrinović threw a bomb at their car; it bounced off the folded cover and exploded under the next car, wounding 16-20 people. The plot seemed to have failed. Afterwards the couple gave up their programme to visit the wounded in hospital, and Governor Oskar Potiorek decided the motorcade should go straight along the Appel Quay, but nobody told the drivers. At the Latin Bridge the two cars in front turned right into Franz Joseph Street on the old route and the Archduke's driver, Leopold Lojka, followed. Potiorek, riding in the car, shouted that it was the wrong way; as Lojka shifted into reverse the car stood still for a few seconds, right where nineteen-year-old Gavrilo Princip was standing, in front of a delicatessen. Princip fired two shots at point-blank range, killing Franz Ferdinand and Sophie. Austria-Hungary blamed Serbian officials and declared war on Serbia on 28 July, one month later; Russia mobilised for Serbia, Germany backed Austria with its "blank cheque", France stood by its alliance with Russia; by 4 August (within a week) Germany, Russia, France, Belgium and Britain were at war. The war killed an estimated 15-22 million people and the Russian, German, Austro-Hungarian and Ottoman empires collapsed. Whether war would have come anyway is debated: many historians say sooner or later there would have been a conflict, others (Clark, Levy and Mulligan) doubt it would have been this one.

- Sophie dead on arrival, Franz Ferdinand ten minutes later: not needed; the script says both were killed.
