## What makes the flight deck read as itself
- **Windscreen:** two big front panes split by a thick dark centre post, two angled side panes, a heavy dark frame.
  At cruise the sky is a deep blue overhead, a pale haze band sits on the horizon and the cloud sea lies far below,
  flat and white, with the horizon very slightly curved.
- **Glareshield:** a dark, padded shelf right under the windscreen running the full width, with the autopilot strip
  in its middle (small amber and green digits) and a red MASTER WARN and amber MASTER CAUTION button in front of
  each pilot.
- **Main panel:** dark blue-grey, six glowing screens in a row (an attitude display with a blue-over-brown ball, a map
  display with green arcs, two engine/warning screens stacked in the middle), three small round standby gauges in
  the centre.
- **Overhead panel:** slopes down toward the windscreen, packed with rows of switches and small lit amber/white
  pushbuttons; it frames the top of every view from behind the seats.
- **Centre pedestal** between the seats with the two thrust levers on top, radio boxes and a trim wheel; the
  pilots' seats are dark blue fabric with a sheepskin-style cover, armrests, and a side console with the side-stick
  or the yoke in front of each pilot.
- **Side windows** at head height beside each seat (sliding window plus a rear one), bright sky in them.
- **Cabin altitude gauge:** a square black bezel with four corner screws, a round black face, white ticks, big white
  numbers in thousands of feet, a green arc for normal and a red limit mark, and a broad white needle; printed
  "CABIN ALTITUDE x1000 FT". On the real aircraft it lives on the overhead pressurisation panel; we put a big one on
  the glareshield centre so the crane can land on it.
- **Quick-donning mask:** a full-face mask, a clear wide visor over the eyes, a grey rubber cup over the nose and
  mouth with the regulator and a red tab in front, a corrugated hose running down to the side console, and a
  harness of flat straps that springs open and grips the head; stowed in a small box on the side console beside
  each seat.

## Light for puppets
- **Windscreen daylight** is the strong source: a cool white directional light from ahead and above, catching the
  pilots' faces and shoulders from the front, a rim on the side toward the glass.
- **Screens and panel lights** are a soft glow; they do not light the puppets.
- **In the emergency:** the red MASTER WARN flashes; it is close and bright enough to flick red on the faces in
  front of it (a red point light whose strength flickers, not a steady sine).

## Chosen for a 9:16 frame
1. Seen from behind and between the seats: windscreen sky and cloud sea in the top half, glareshield and glowing
   screens across the middle, the overhead panel edge framing the top.
2. The pilots in profile facing the centre, so their faces read; the seat backs hide their bodies from behind, as
   in real photos taken from the jump seat.
3. One big story instrument at a time on the glareshield centre (CABIN ALT gauge, then the altimeter readout), drawn
   about twice real size, above the caption band.
4. The red MASTER WARN buttons on the glareshield edge for the dive: their flash reads at once.
