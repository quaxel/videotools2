# Research: The Day Meat Fell From the Sky in Kentucky

## Mechanism

On 3 March 1876, under a clear sky, small flakes of what looked like fresh meat fell near the house of Allen Crouch,
two or three miles from Olympian Springs in southern Bath County, Kentucky. Mrs. Crouch was in the yard making soap
when it began; she said the pieces fell like large snowflakes, mostly about 2 inches square, one 3-4 inches. They
covered a strip about 100 yards long and 50 wide and stuck to the fences. Two men tasted some and thought it was mutton
or venison. Explanations disagreed: Leopold Brandeis called it nostoc (a cyanobacterium that swells into jelly after
rain — but the sky was clear); histologists found lung, muscle and cartilage tissue in the samples. In 1876
Dr. L. D. Kastenbine (Louisville Medical News) proposed that vultures flying overhead disgorged their food, which
would explain the mix of tissues; black and turkey vultures in Kentucky do vomit to lighten themselves or when
threatened. It remains the most cited explanation but was never proven, and no vultures were reported that day.
A preserved sample is still at Transylvania University's Moosnick museum in Lexington.

Name note: the husband is Allen Crouch in every source; the wife is called Mrs. Crouch in the 1876 report and
"Rebecca" or "Mary" in later retellings. The script says only "Mrs. Crouch".

## Claims

- The fall happened in Kentucky in 1876, eighteen seventy-six (3 March 1876, Bath County, near Olympian Springs) — https://afflictor.com/2013/08/13/a-brief-note-from-1876-about-a-meat-shower/
- The sky was perfectly clear when it fell — https://afflictor.com/2013/08/13/a-brief-note-from-1876-about-a-meat-shower/
- Mrs. Crouch was outside in her yard making soap when it started falling around her — https://afflictor.com/2013/08/13/a-brief-note-from-1876-about-a-meat-shower/
- Two men tasted it and said it was mutton or venison — https://afflictor.com/2013/08/13/a-brief-note-from-1876-about-a-meat-shower/
- Pieces mostly about 2 inches square, one 3-4 inches; strip about 100 by 50 yards — https://afflictor.com/2013/08/13/a-brief-note-from-1876-about-a-meat-shower/
- Kastenbine proposed in 1876 that vultures disgorged it overhead; theory, not proven — https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/running-ponies/the-great-kentucky-meat-shower-mystery-unwound-by-projectile-vulture-vomit/
- Samples held lung, muscle and cartilage; nostoc theory conflicts with the clear sky — https://www.scientificamerican.com/blog/running-ponies/the-great-kentucky-meat-shower-mystery-unwound-by-projectile-vulture-vomit/
- Vulture vomit is presented as "the thinking", not proven — https://www.sciencefocus.com/nature/kentucky-meat-shower
- Mrs. Crouch's account mentions a clear sky and "no warning of any kind" but no birds; the vulture idea is a later,
  second-hand suggestion ("an old Ohio farmer" via Kastenbine) — https://www.mentalfloss.com/history/time-it-rained-flesh-kentucky
- No source says birds were seen, and none states outright that "no one reported birds": the accurate wording is that
  the witness accounts never mention birds — (absence across all three sources above)
