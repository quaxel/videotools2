# Research: Why some European oven clocks ran six minutes slow in 2018

Idea: ideation DB idea-003 (doc-idea-003). Limitation respected: not every clock was affected; only devices that
keep time by counting the mains frequency (oven clocks, radio alarm clocks, heating timers). Quartz/phone clocks are not.

## Mechanism

The continental European grid runs at a nominal 50 Hz: the alternating current swings back and forth fifty times a
second. Some plug-in clocks (oven clocks, radio alarm clocks, heating-programme timers) do not have their own quartz
crystal; they keep time by counting those mains cycles, so fifty cycles = one second. The frequency stays at 50 Hz only
while power fed in matches power drawn. From mid-January to the beginning of March 2018 a political dispute between the
Serbian and Kosovar grid operators left Kosovo's control area short (Kosovo's operator could not buy enough supply and
the Serbian operator, which should have balanced it, did not, "for political reasons"). 113 GWh went missing, the
average frequency of the shared synchronous area (25 countries, Spain to Turkey) sat around 49.996 Hz, and every
cycle-counting clock ran slightly slow; the tiny loss per second added up to close to six minutes. ENTSO-E confirmed
the deviation stopped on 8 March; during March the operators put extra energy into the system, kept the average at
50.01 Hz, and by 3 April the clocks were back on time. Blame is disputed (each operator blamed the other). The script's line
"Kosovo's grid was short of power, and Serbia's operator didn't fill the gap" follows National Geographic's account
(Kosovo's operator could not buy enough supply; balancing should have been done by the Serbian operator, which for
political reasons did not); the video stages the two operators pointing at each other and the SERBIA GRID operator
with arms crossed at the GAP. The operators are generic puppets with no dialogue.
